Xinglü 行旅 — five questions, one verdict you can check

An entry-decision tool for first-time visitors to China. It states which published rule covers your case, when that rule expires, what you must still satisfy, and where the official text is. It is not a marketing page: what it cannot answer is listed inside the product, not just here.

1. Position

Xinglü answers three questions a first-time visitor cannot answer themselves: can I get in, by when must I act, and what happens in my first hour on the ground. It outputs a verdict plus the evidence chain, then hands the user back to the official page. It does not book, does not act as a visa agency, and does not draw a map it cannot load.

Who it is for

  • Primary: adults holding a foreign passport, travelling to China for tourism or short business, working in English, unfamiliar with Chinese official portals.
  • Secondary: Chinese hosts arranging a visit for foreign friends; editors who need to cite the policy line rather than paraphrase it.
  • Not for: anyone needing an agency to file for them, anyone needing live seat or ticket inventory, anyone needing immigration legal advice.

3. Flow

Not a form — a conversation. One question at a time, chips plus free text, every question skippable, every answer changeable. A sixth question appears only when it can change the outcome: whether you hold a through ticket to a third country is asked when the 30-day list does not cover you and the 240-hour channel might.

#QuestionDecides
1Where are you going?Weather coordinates, location card, seasonal note
2Roughly when do you arrive?Expiry test, action timeline (relative answers produce an estimated window and say so)
3How many nights?30-day and 240-hour caps
4Which passport?Channel membership, per-country expiry. Used for the check only; never stored off-device
5What matters most?Which extra findings appear
+Through ticket to a third country?240-hour channel only, only when it can flip the verdict

5. The expiry cliff — the feature that general tools cannot have

As of the 2025-11-03 extension notice, most of the 30-day list is published as running to 2026-12-31 24:00 — 56 days from the date this pack was checked. Official pages carry three different counts (54 / 55 / 57) for transit coverage, and the policy changed five times in a year (72/144h→240h in 2024-12, 55 countries 2025-06, 65 ports 2025-11-05, UK added 2026-02-17, 57 countries 2026-08-20).

The engine therefore does not pretend to be current — it states the date it was checked. And it does not infer law: the announcement gives an end date and is silent on stays continuing past it, so a crossing trip is flagged window_conflict with both dates shown, not a prediction.

Concretely, a German traveller arriving 2026-12-28 for 7 nights gets: expiry 2026-12-31, trip end 2027-01-04, straddles: true, and the instruction to look for an extension notice before booking anything non-refundable. A Russian traveller in January 2027 gets no conflict, because their stated expiry is 2027-12-31.

7. Trust mechanics (implemented, not described)

  • Four-part citation on every claim: source name (EN + 中文), direct official link, queried or page date, scope. Policy items add "official snapshot, not legal advice".
  • Five-state adapter: ok / failed / snapshot / link / estimate. Live calls use no-store, 8s AbortController timeout, and on failure display the reason — never a previous value.
  • Confidence tiers are visible: a not officially verified badge is rendered in the UI, not hidden in a footnote.
  • Staleness degradation: past a 30-day review window the engine stops concluding and returns links only.
  • Banned phrasing: guaranteed entry, visa approved, definitely on time, and never presenting an estimate as live. Asserted in tests, including that negations ("not a guarantee of entry") remain legal.
  • No map it cannot load: coordinates, three hand-offs (geo:, Amap, Google Maps) and an explicit note that Amap's web service returns INVALID_USER_KEY without a key.
  • Offline first: one self-contained HTML file, no external assets, opens from file://, prints. Live blocks keep the values captured at export time with their timestamp.
  • Privacy: passport country and itinerary only; no passport number, no account, no network call except the two data APIs, everything in device-local storage.

Per-country expiry on the transit listNot assertedNo single official page consolidates it; the conflict is shown rather than resolved by guess.

Payments, accounts, syncNot done by designTrust is the asset; taking money or identity data would spend it.
LLM answersNot in the determination pathA model may rephrase this evidence pack one day; it will not decide eligibility or invent a rule.
